## How to read this chart

The chart models a $25 bitcoin purchase every Monday, starting January 6, 2020, using that day's average price. Each purchase adds sats to the total.

The line shows sats accumulated, rather than their dollar value. It rises because the model adds bitcoin and makes no withdrawals.

## What the slope tells you

When bitcoin's price is lower, $25 buys more sats and the line climbs faster. When the price is higher, it buys fewer sats and the line climbs more slowly.

That is how a regular purchase works, often called dollar-cost averaging. The schedule stays steady while the amount of bitcoin purchased changes.

## What the chart cannot tell you

It does not establish that weekly buying beats buying all at once. That depends on the dates and prices. It does not predict returns or remove the risk of loss.

The figures illustrate purchases at historical average prices. Real purchases can differ because of timing, fees, and spread.
