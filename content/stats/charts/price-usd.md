## How to read this chart

Each point is the daily average price of one bitcoin in US dollars across major exchanges. It records the price for that day, so it can differ from the live price on the homepage or your exchange.

The vertical scale is logarithmic. Equal distances show equal multiples: a rise from $100 to $1,000 takes the same space as $10,000 to $100,000. That makes the early years readable alongside recent prices.

The halving markers show when the amount of new bitcoin awarded for each block was cut in half. Their position does not prove that a halving caused a price move. The record-high tag refers to this daily average series, rather than every exchange's highest trade.

Use the table to compare dates and amounts. A small-looking dip on a long chart can still be a large loss. [Why bitcoin has a price](https://fastfactsforsats.com/money/why-bitcoin-has-a-price/) explains the forces behind the price; the chart records their past result.
