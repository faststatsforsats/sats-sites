## How to read this chart

When bitcoin's dollar price rises, one dollar buys fewer sats and this line falls. When the price drops, a dollar buys more sats and the line rises. The calculation is 100,000,000 divided by the bitcoin price in dollars.

The vertical scale is logarithmic. Equal distances represent equal multiples: a move from 1,000 to 10,000 sats takes the same space as 10,000 to 100,000. This keeps Bitcoin's early years and recent history readable on one chart.

Each point uses the daily average price across major exchanges, so it can differ from a live quote. The line includes long stretches when a dollar bought more sats as bitcoin fell. Past movement does not tell you where it goes next.

[Why bitcoin has a price](https://fastfactsforsats.com/money/why-bitcoin-has-a-price/) explains what moves the dollar price. For a simpler annual view, see [Sats per dollar, every January since 2011](/history/). To check any amount, use the [sats converter](/tools/sats-converter/).
