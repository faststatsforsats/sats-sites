## How to read this chart

This line is the bitcoin price turned upside down. When the price in dollars goes up, a dollar buys fewer sats, so the line falls; when the price drops, the line rises. Drawing it this way puts the dollar on the chart as the thing being measured, which is how most people actually meet bitcoin: not as a coin they own, but as a price they pay.

The axis is logarithmic, so equal distances up the chart mean equal multiples, not equal amounts. That is the only way to fit 2011 and today on one page. On January 1, 2011, a dollar bought more than 300 million sats, because a whole bitcoin cost about 30 cents. By January 2014 it bought about 137,000, by January 2021 about 3,450, and the table above gives today's figure. A plain axis would show a cliff in the first two years and a flat line ever after.

Three things to keep in mind. First, the figure is a daily average across exchanges, not a single trade, so it can differ from the number on any one app by a percent or so. Second, the line has gone up for long stretches too: 2014, 2018, and 2022 each saw a dollar buy two to four times more sats within a year, and the chart shows every one of those stretches at full size. Third, nothing on this page says where the line goes next; it records where it has been.
