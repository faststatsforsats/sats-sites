Your first purchase has one job: teach you how buying bitcoin works. Keep it small enough that you can concentrate on the steps.

You do not need a whole bitcoin. Sats are its smallest unit, and $25 buys [[live:sats-for:25:usd]] right now ([[live:when]]).

Have your government ID, bank details, email address, and an authenticator app ready. The form may take about 20 minutes. Identity checks and bank transfers can take longer.

## <span class="step">Step 1</span> Choose a service

Compare the total cost, availability in your state, and how you will move bitcoin to your own wallet.

| Service | Focus | Recurring buys | After the purchase | Availability |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kraken | Multiple assets | Yes; check fee and spread | Held in account until withdrawal | Check country and state |
| Coinbase | Multiple assets | Yes; review preview | Held in account until withdrawal | Check country and state |
| River | Bitcoin | Fee-free after first 7 days | Held in account until withdrawal | US; check state |
| Bitcoin Well | Bitcoin | Country-specific options | Sent to your own wallet after settlement | US and Canada; check terms |

Check current fees before ordering. A low trading fee may still come with a spread, payment fee, or withdrawal charge.

## <span class="step">Step 2</span> Open and secure the account

Follow the service's identity check. It commonly asks for personal details, an ID photo, and sometimes a selfie.

Use a unique password. Turn on app-based two-factor authentication, a passkey, or a hardware security key where supported. Save the recovery codes somewhere safe. [Privacy basics for bitcoiners](/secure/privacy-basics/) explains the account protections.

## <span class="step">Step 3</span> Link your bank

A bank transfer is often cheaper than a card payment, but the service may hold withdrawals while it settles. Check the fee and the waiting period.

If you use a card, check whether the issuer adds a cash-advance fee or blocks the purchase.

## <span class="step">Step 4</span> Preview a small purchase

Before confirming, check the dollars you will pay, the fees, and the bitcoin you will receive. If the app offers a sats display, turn it on.

For reference, 0.00029 BTC equals 29,000 sats. Different label, same bitcoin.

## <span class="step">Step 5</span> Check the cost and save the record

Use the [sats converter](https://faststatsforsats.com/tools/sats-converter/) on Fast Stats to compare your purchase with the market price at roughly the same time. The difference can reflect fees, spread, and price movement. It is a useful check, rather than an exact fee calculation.

Save the confirmation, date, dollars paid, sats received, and fees. Start the record now, while it is easy.

## <span class="step">Step 6</span> Choose where to keep it

An exchange holds the keys to bitcoin in your account. You depend on the company to let you withdraw it.

A wallet you control gives you the keys and the responsibility for protecting them. Read [how a bitcoin wallet works](https://fastfactsforsats.com/basics/how-a-wallet-works/), then [how to store sats safely](/store/how-to-store-sats-safely/) before moving funds.

## If you want a recurring buy

Choose a day and an amount that fits your budget. A regular purchase reduces the need to decide when to buy. It does not prevent losses.

The [$25 a week chart](https://faststatsforsats.com/charts/twenty-five-a-week/) shows one historical example. [Your first 100,000 sats](/first-100k-sats/) turns the process into a checklist.

## Keep the tax record

In the US, buying bitcoin with dollars generally creates no taxable gain. Selling, spending, or exchanging it later can create a gain or loss to report. Keep your purchase cost and fees. [Do I owe taxes on sats?](https://fastfactsforsats.com/rules/taxes-on-sats/) explains the basics.

A small first purchase is enough to learn the screen. You can decide what comes next after that.
