Stacking sats means gradually adding small amounts of bitcoin to your holdings.

At today's price, $25 buys [[live:sats-for:25:usd]] ([[live:when]]).

You do not need to buy a whole bitcoin.

You simply add sats.

Then add some more.

Hence the word stacking.

## Where did the phrase come from?

A sat is one hundred-millionth of a bitcoin.

Around 2019, the phrase "stay humble, stack sats" became common on Bitcoin podcasts and social media.

The idea was deliberately simple.

Forget trying to look rich.

Accumulate something you believe has value.

## How people stack sats

There are several common ways.

### Regular purchases

Someone might buy $10, $25, or $100 of bitcoin every week or month.

This is commonly called dollar-cost averaging.

Instead of trying to guess the perfect day to buy, the purchase happens on a schedule. Fast Stats shows what a fixed amount bought every January since 2011, for example [$25 in sats](https://faststatsforsats.com/sats/25-usd/).

### Earning sats

Some people earn sats through:

- Cashback programs
- Rewards
- Tips
- Work paid in bitcoin

No purchase is required.

### Round-ups

Some services round purchases to the next dollar and use the difference to buy bitcoin.

Small amounts quietly accumulate.

## The second word matters too

Stacking usually implies keeping.

Someone who regularly buys and immediately sells is trading.

Someone stacking sats generally plans to hold them.

That eventually raises another question:

Where should those sats be stored?

[Fast Acts for Sats](https://fastactsforsats.com/store/how-to-store-sats-safely/) covers wallets, security, and self-custody.

## What stacking sats is not

It is not a promise of wealth.

It is not a trading system.

And it says nothing about next year's price. The [sats per dollar chart](https://faststatsforsats.com/charts/sats-per-dollar/) shows what happened; it says nothing about what happens next.

It is simply a habit.

Big stacks are built the same way big walls are built: one piece at a time.
