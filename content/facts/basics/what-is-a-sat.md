A sat is the smallest unit of bitcoin.

1 sat = 0.00000001 BTC

<!-- quote: sats-in-a-bitcoin -->

There are 100,000,000 sats in one bitcoin.

<!-- /quote: sats-in-a-bitcoin -->

Right now, one U.S. dollar buys [[live:sats-per-dollar]] ([[live:when]]).

That is why everyday amounts of bitcoin are usually easier to understand in sats than in long strings of decimals.

## Where did the name come from?

Sat is short for satoshi, named after Satoshi Nakamoto, the pseudonymous author of the 2008 Bitcoin white paper.

The original paper never called the unit a satoshi. Bitcoin software simply counted these tiny units from the beginning.

Around 2010, Bitcoin users started calling them satoshis.

The name stuck.

## What is a sat worth?

A sat does not have a separate price.

It is simply one hundred-millionth of whatever a bitcoin is worth.

At [[live:price]] per bitcoin:

100,000 sats = [[live:money-for:100000]]

One sat is worth only a tiny fraction of a cent.

That number changes whenever bitcoin's price changes. The [sats per dollar chart](https://faststatsforsats.com/charts/sats-per-dollar/) on Fast Stats shows every day since 2011, and the [sats converter](https://faststatsforsats.com/tools/sats-converter/) turns any amount into sats.

## Why bother with sats?

Because decimals get ugly fast.

A $5 purchase might look like:

0.00005 BTC

The same amount might be:

5,000 sats

Same value. Much easier number.

There is another benefit.

Thinking in sats reminds you that owning bitcoin does not mean buying an entire bitcoin.

<!-- quote: own-part -->

You can own 10,000 sats, 100,000 sats, or 10 million sats.

<!-- /quote: own-part -->

A pizza does not require buying the whole restaurant.

## Where are sats used?

You will see sats in:

- Bitcoin wallets
- Lightning payments
- Transaction fees
- Rewards programs
- Tips
- Small purchases

Most wallets let you switch between BTC and sats.

For everyday amounts, sats usually make more sense.

Bitcoin is the headline number. Sats are where most people actually live.
