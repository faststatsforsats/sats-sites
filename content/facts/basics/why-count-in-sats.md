Most people ask:

How many dollars is my bitcoin worth?

Counting in sats flips the question:

How many sats is my dollar worth?

Nothing magical happens.

You are simply looking at the other side of the price.

## Every price has two sides

If a dozen eggs costs $3, we usually say:

Eggs cost $3.

But another equally valid question is:

How many sats does $3 buy?

Now you have two moving pieces:

- The dollar value of the eggs
- The dollar value of bitcoin

That creates another way to measure the same purchase.

## The dollar is not a fixed yardstick

The Consumer Price Index measures changes in the cost of a basket of goods.

The CPI was 220.2 in January 2011 and [[live:cpi]].

In simple terms, the same basket of goods became considerably more expensive in dollars.

Most of us experience this slowly.

A dollar still looks like a dollar.

It just buys less.

## Bitcoin uses a different supply system

The number of sats grows according to a schedule built into Bitcoin.

Nobody can decide next Tuesday that the world needs 20 percent more sats.

The issuance rate is known in advance, slows with each halving, and eventually reaches zero.

That does not guarantee bitcoin's purchasing power.

It simply makes its supply rules very different from those of government currency.

## Eggs tell the story

In August 2016, a dozen large eggs averaged about $1.46.

In August 2026, they averaged about $2.27.

That is an increase of roughly 56 percent in dollars.

Measured in sats, however, the price fell dramatically because bitcoin rose much faster than eggs during that period. The [eggs in sats chart](https://faststatsforsats.com/charts/eggs-in-sats/) on Fast Stats draws the whole history, and the [everyday prices](https://faststatsforsats.com/value/everyday-prices/) page does the same for a whole basket every month.

Both statements can be true.

That is the point.

A price is always one thing measured against another. Change the ruler, and the picture changes.

## But sats do not always win

Bitcoin prices fall too.

In years such as 2014, 2018, and 2022, a dollar bought substantially more sats by the end of the year than it did at the beginning. The [year-by-year table](https://faststatsforsats.com/history/) on Fast Stats shows every January since 2011.

Counting in sats is not a prediction.

It is another lens.

And sometimes the view is beautiful. Sometimes somebody forgot to wash the window.

## What counting in sats is not

It is not a prediction that the dollar will fail.

It is not an argument that everyone should spend bitcoin.

It is not investment advice.

It is simply another way to measure value.

The ruler matters more than most people realize.
