Bitcoin is often called digital gold.

There is a reason.

Both are scarce.

Neither depends on a company's earnings.

Both are commonly held as stores of value.

But the similarities end quickly.

## Gold and Bitcoin side by side

| | Gold | Bitcoin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maximum supply | No fixed maximum | Roughly 21 million |
| New supply | Mining adds more each year | Issuance follows a fixed schedule |
| Verification | Testing or trusted dealer | Bitcoin nodes |
| Movement | Shipping and custody | Digital transaction |
| Divisibility | Ounces, grams, coins | 100 million sats per bitcoin |
| Storage | Safe, vault, custodian | Private keys or custodian |
| History | Thousands of years | Since 2009 |

## Gold is scarce

Gold is difficult and expensive to find.

That limits supply.

But supply can respond to price.

If gold becomes more valuable, companies have a stronger incentive to explore, mine harder-to-reach deposits, and develop new technology.

More gold can eventually reach the market.

## Bitcoin is scarce differently

Bitcoin's issuance does not respond to price.

Bitcoin could trade for $1,000 or $10 million.

The supply schedule does not care.

New issuance continues according to the same rules.

That makes bitcoin's scarcity mathematically fixed in a way gold's is not. [The 21 million bitcoin limit](/money/the-21-million-cap/) explains the schedule.

## Gold has something Bitcoin cannot manufacture

History.

Gold has been valued across civilizations for thousands of years.

Bitcoin began in 2009.

That says nothing about what either one is worth tomorrow.

It tells us that gold has a much longer record.

Bitcoin has a much shorter one.

## Bitcoin is easier to move

Moving a significant amount of physical gold can require:

- Shipping
- Insurance
- Security
- Vaults
- Trusted intermediaries

Bitcoin can move across the world with a transaction.

That convenience brings a different responsibility.

Lose control of your private keys and you may lose the bitcoin permanently.

Gold can be stolen.

Bitcoin can be forgotten.

Different asset. Different headache.

## Which is better?

That is not what this site decides.

They solve some of the same problems in very different ways.

Gold offers thousands of years of history.

Bitcoin offers mathematically fixed scarcity and digital portability.

The market gets to argue about the rest. The [gold in sats chart](https://faststatsforsats.com/charts/gold-in-sats/) on Fast Stats shows how that argument has gone month by month since 2011, and the [comparisons page](https://faststatsforsats.com/value/comparisons/) puts gold, a new home, and the S&P 500 in sats on one table.

Old gold has wrinkles. Young Bitcoin has bruises. Both have survived a few fights.
