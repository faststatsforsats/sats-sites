Bitcoin has a price for a simple reason:

People buy it and people sell it.

## There is no price inside Bitcoin

Bitcoin software does not know what bitcoin is worth.

It knows:

- How many coins exist
- Who controls them
- Whether transactions follow the rules

That is it.

The market supplies the price.

## So who decides what bitcoin is worth?

Nobody.

And everybody.

Thousands of buyers and sellers place orders across exchanges around the world.

When a buyer and seller agree, a trade happens.

The price is simply the level where people are currently willing to exchange bitcoin for dollars, euros, yen, or another currency.

## What affects the price?

People consider things such as:

- Bitcoin's [fixed supply](/money/the-21-million-cap/)
- Adoption
- Regulation
- Interest rates
- Economic conditions
- Market sentiment
- Access to exchanges and investment products

There is no single formula.

At the end of the day, price is the result of millions of decisions.

## Why is bitcoin so volatile?

Bitcoin trades 24 hours a day.

There are no nights.

No weekends.

No closing bell.

Its supply also cannot suddenly increase when demand rises.

Combine that with a relatively young global market and constant news, and price swings can be large.

Bitcoin has had years when its dollar price more than tripled.

It has also had years when it fell by more than half.

Both are part of the record. The [price chart since 2011](https://faststatsforsats.com/charts/price-usd/) on Fast Stats shows every one of them, and the [Fear and Greed gauge](https://faststatsforsats.com/charts/fear-greed/) tracks the market's mood day by day.

## Is there one official bitcoin price?

No.

Different exchanges can show slightly different prices at the same moment.

The live figure on this site comes from CoinGecko, which combines data from multiple markets.

The historical daily charts use blockchain.com data.

The numbers are usually close, but they do not have to match exactly.

There is no official bitcoin price.

There is only the latest agreement between buyers and sellers.

A market price is not an opinion. It is where two opinions just shook hands.
