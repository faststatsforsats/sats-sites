In the United States, simply buying and holding bitcoin is generally not a taxable event.

But selling it, spending it, swapping it, or earning it can create tax consequences.

The IRS generally treats bitcoin as property, not as ordinary currency.

That one rule explains most of what follows.

## Buying bitcoin

Buy bitcoin with dollars and keep it?

Generally, no gain or loss has occurred yet.

Your purchase price becomes your cost basis.

## Selling bitcoin

When you sell, compare:

What you received

with

Your cost basis

The difference is generally a capital gain or capital loss.

## Spending bitcoin

This surprises people.

Buying something with bitcoin is generally treated as disposing of the bitcoin.

So if your sats increased in value between the day you acquired them and the day you bought that cup of coffee, you may have a taxable gain.

Yes, even coffee can have paperwork.

The tax code has never been accused of being shy.

## Moving bitcoin between your own wallets

Generally, moving bitcoin between wallets or accounts that you own is not a sale.

You still own the same asset.

Keep good records so you can document what moved where.

## Swapping bitcoin for another cryptocurrency

That is generally considered a disposal of the bitcoin.

A gain or loss may result.

## Earning bitcoin

Bitcoin received for:

- Work
- Mining
- Rewards
- Certain other activities

may be taxable as income based on its dollar value when received.

That value usually becomes your basis for calculating a later gain or loss.

## Quick reference

| What you do | General U.S. tax treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Buy and hold | Generally no gain or loss |
| Transfer between your own wallets | Generally no gain or loss |
| Sell for dollars | Capital gain or loss |
| Spend bitcoin | Capital gain or loss may apply |
| Swap for another asset | Capital gain or loss may apply |
| Receive bitcoin as income | Generally taxable income |
| Receive a gift | Special basis rules apply |
| Donate to charity | Special deduction rules may apply |

## Short-term versus long-term

Hold bitcoin for one year or less before selling and a gain is generally short-term.

Hold it for more than one year and a gain is generally long-term.

The tax rates can be different.

## Keep records

For every acquisition and disposal, keep:

- Date
- Amount of bitcoin
- Dollar value
- Cost basis
- Transaction details
- Wallet or account involved

People who buy bitcoin regularly can end up with many individual tax lots.

That gets complicated quickly.

## Forms

Bitcoin gains and losses may appear on:

- Form 8949
- Schedule D

Other bitcoin income may appear elsewhere depending on how it was earned.

U.S. brokers may also issue Form 1099-DA for certain digital asset transactions.

## One final caution

Tax rules change.

States have their own rules.

Your personal facts matter.

This page explains the general U.S. framework. It is not individual tax advice.

Before making a significant tax decision, talk with a qualified tax professional.

Bitcoin may be decentralized. The IRS remains impressively centralized.
